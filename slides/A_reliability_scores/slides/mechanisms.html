Why rules fail

Three mechanisms

1 · EASY PICKS

Absolute scores reward them

Copying a prior c scores a Brier of c(1−c): 0.029 on a 97% event, 0.25 on a 50% event.

2 · BROKEN BOUND

Ratios escape the zero-skill bound

For sums and means of proper-score differences the expected gain is at most zero. The Brier skill score's weights depend on outcomes.

3 · BIASED BASELINE

The bound needs a calibrated crowd

With a predictable crowd bias, a source can undo it. IG-SUM climbs from 11.5 to 81.1 by flooding about 990 claims.

Mechanism 2 holds when claims and probabilities are fixed before outcomes are revealed, which our cheater satisfies. The bound is on expected score, not expected percentile: a mean over 10 claims has high variance, which is how a variance gamble lifts the mean information gain to 27.8.
